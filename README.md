# Johnson Service Group (LSE:JSG)

Valuation and transaction analysis of a UK textile rental and workwear
business, built from a three-statement model on Capital IQ filings
FY2021 to FY2025, forecast to FY2030. All three documents run off the same
model core.

## Documents

JSG_Equity_Pitch.pdf

Long thesis. Target 190p against a 133p share price at 11 September
2026, on four valuation methods with a 140p to 150p floor. The argument
is that the market is valuing JSG on revenue, which is flat, when the
business should be valued on cash generation, which is inflecting: H1
2026 revenue grew 0.2% while EPS grew 8.7%, and a completed £200m
capital programme means capex is now falling.

JSG_LBO_Analysis.pdf

Leveraged buyout affordability analysis. A sponsor can pay roughly 140p
at a 20.2% IRR with no multiple expansion. The conclusion is negative:
a public board would want 170p or more, so there is no price at which a
financial buyer and the board overlap. The binding constraint is debt
capacity rather than operating performance. Entry leverage of 3.0x on
EBITDA is 5.39x on EBITDA less maintenance capex, because roughly £74m
of £170m EBITDA is rental stock replacement.

JSG_Credit_Assessment.pdf

One-page credit assessment. Altman Z-score of 2.90 places JSG in the
grey zone; a Merton structural model puts it 6.1 standard deviations
from default. The two disagree because the Z-score penalises asset
intensity, and the covenant test is the one that binds: EBITDA can fall
62% before the leverage covenant is breached.

## Method

Segment-level revenue and margin forecasts across HORECA and Workwear.
Discounted cash flow, trading comparables and a leveraged buyout
affordability analysis run from the same forecast. The LBO uses an
amortising debt schedule with a cash sweep and circular interest on
average balances.

The underlying Excel model is not published. Available on request.

## Limitations

The LBO uses a single debt tranche with no covenant modelling and no
management equity, and taxes levered cash flow.

Comparable company multiples are distorted by capitalised rental stock.
JSG screens at 4.6x EV/EBITDA against a 6.7x peer median, but the
asset-light peers carry no equivalent to £95.7m of annual rental stock
depreciation. The forward P/E comparison is the honest one.

Terminal value is 76% of enterprise value in the DCF, so the valuation
is sensitive to the terminal assumptions. A sensitivity grid across
WACC and terminal growth is included in the pitch.
